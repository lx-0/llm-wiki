## What this does

The T05 deliverable acknowledges a reality of LLM-emission testing: emission quality cannot be CI-tested deterministically. The canary is a human-in-the-loop step. Three substrates worth canary-running:

1. **Synthetic fixture first** — `tests/fixtures/jamie/2026-04-15--canary-q1-review--abc.md` from T04. Cheapest dry-run; the truth-set is explicit in the fixture's `## Expected extraction` block. Confirms the prompt rules carry through end-to-end on a controlled input.
2. **One live jamie meeting from lxw vault** — operator picks a recent jamie file with ≥2 attendees and an explicit commitment in the transcript. Real-shape stress test; reveals fuzzy-substrate issues that synthetic fixtures hide.
3. **One live gmeet meeting from lxw vault** — same drill with a Gemini-transcribed meeting. Different speaker-label shape and different LLM-output style; reveals substrate-specific drift.

For each canary, the runbook tells the operator:

- The exact `wiki compile` invocation
- What entity pages should be created or modified
- What the `## Action Items` content should look like (Obsidian-Tasks syntax with `📅` etc.)
- What the `## Open Threads` content should look like
- What the Timeline entry should look like
- The grep commands to verify each of the above
- When to declare PASS / PASS-with-caveats / FAIL
- When PASS-with-caveats: which prompt-iteration knob to turn (extraction recall vs precision; routing ambiguity)

Pass/fail philosophy: extraction recall matters but precision matters more. False positives (hallucinated commitments) are worse than false negatives (missed real ones). The runbook biases toward "did the LLM keep the spec, or did it improvise?".
